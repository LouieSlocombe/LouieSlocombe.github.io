Sustainable polymers

How can we connect a polymer’s chemistry to its useful properties and its fate after use?

Current focus · Birmingham

Polymers are long molecules made from repeating units. Their chemistry and the way their chains pack together influence how a material stretches, softens, and interacts with water. I am interested in how we can use that molecular picture to design materials with useful properties and better options at the end of their life.

At Birmingham, I am developing databases, machine-learning methods, and molecular simulations to study polymer properties and biodegradability. This is ongoing research: the aim is to connect evidence from experiments with models that can help guide which materials to investigate next.

Learning from the evidence

Useful predictions need useful data. Published measurements are spread across articles, tables, and figures, often with different experimental conditions. I build tools to bring those records together while retaining the information needed to interpret them.

My recent work includes PaperMinerToolkit, developed with the Scanlon Materials Theory Group, for collecting papers and extracting structured information using text and vision models. figrecover recovers numerical data from plotted curves. These tools help with data collection; checking the extracted values, units, and experimental context remains part of the research.

Following chains in a simulation

I develop openmmpolymer to build, pack, and simulate polymer melts and blends. It connects a description of a repeating unit to calculations of structure, mechanical behaviour, and the glass transition, where a material changes between glassy and more mobile behaviour.

Alongside this, I am exploring how molecular structure, water access, and reaction pathways influence polymer breakdown, including routes relevant to chemical recycling. Biodegradation and chemical recycling involve different processes; understanding both helps us ask more precise questions about a material’s fate.

Connecting models to experiments

A simulation follows a model over a limited time, and a machine-learning prediction depends on the examples it has seen. I therefore work on checking sampling, comparing calculations with measurements, and assessing where predictions are reliable. My wider simulation-methods work supports this effort.

The public tools are openmmpolymer, PaperMinerToolkit, and figrecover. The code catalogue gives more detail on their scope.
