Molecular simulation & quantum dynamics

How do we follow reactions accurately while keeping their surroundings in the picture?

Methods & research software

A chemical reaction may involve only a few atoms, but the water, protein, or material around them can change its outcome. I develop computational methods and software to connect these scales: the detailed physics of a reaction and the motion of its surroundings.

This work supports my studies of DNA and polymers, while producing tools that can be used in other molecular systems.

A detailed reaction inside a larger system

Quantum mechanics describes electrons rearranging as bonds change. Applying that level of detail to an entire protein or a large material can be expensive. QM/MM combines a quantum-mechanical calculation for the region of interest with a simpler molecular-mechanics model for its environment.

My openmmqmmm package connects ORCA and OpenMM for these calculations. It is derived from the ASH modelling program and also provides routes to use machine-learning models for atomic interactions. reactiontools helps find and analyse the paths between molecular arrangements, including the energy barriers that make some reactions slow.

When nuclei behave quantum mechanically

Hydrogen nuclei are light enough that a classical picture of moving particles can miss important behaviour. They can tunnel through an energy barrier, and changing hydrogen to its heavier isotope, deuterium, can change a reaction rate. Those differences provide ways to investigate the mechanism.

I develop HEOM.jl to follow quantum motion while accounting for interaction with an environment. openmmnqe brings methods for quantum nuclei into OpenMM, while nqetools supports setup and analysis around i-PI calculations. Each offers a different balance of physical detail and computational cost.

Making larger calculations useful

I also work on machine-learning models of atomic forces, efficient calculations, and the preparation needed before a simulation can begin. py-chargemol analyses electron density to assign atomic charges and related quantities; vanderfast calculates dispersion interactions, the weak attractions that matter when molecules pack together.

Speed is useful when it lets us study more configurations or longer times. I am equally interested in checking that the model, numerical settings, and sampled structures support the conclusions drawn from a calculation.
