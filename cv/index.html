Curriculum vitae

Louie Slocombe

Computational physicist & theoretical chemist

l.slocombe@bham.ac.uk

My research has taken me from quantum effects in DNA to chemical complexity and the origins of biochemistry. I use computational chemistry, physics, and machine learning to explore these questions. At the University of Birmingham, I'm now applying those methods to sustainable polymers, particularly the challenge of predicting biodegradability.

Research & professional experience

  • Jun 2026 – to Present

    Group Leader Research Fellow in Computational Chemistry and AI/ML for Sustainable Polymers

    University of Birmingham

    School of Chemistry · Scanlon Materials Theory Group

    Birmingham, UK

    I work with David Scanlon on computational chemistry and AI/ML for sustainable polymers, focusing on how to predict biodegradability.

  • Apr 2024 – to Apr 2026

    Research Lab Manager and Postdoctoral Researcher

    Arizona State University

    Beyond Center for Fundamental Concepts in Science

    Tempe, Arizona, USA

    Working with Sara Walker, I used machine learning, chemical complexity, and graph theory to explore the origins of biochemistry, chemical space, and protein folding. Alongside the research, I managed the lab and supported its researchers.

    • Built models and databases informed by physics and chemistry using PyTorch, scikit-learn, and ESMFold. Used molecular dynamics, quantum chemistry, and machine-learning interatomic potentials to study polymers, proteins, and DNA/RNA.
    • Helped secure a $1.5 million NASA ROSES grant through a substantial contribution to the proposal. Developed the Science and Technical Management section, prepared the Facilities & Equipment and Open Science Data Management Plan sections, and drafted the budget. Planned and delivered two-thirds of the grant workload.
    • Mentored, supervised, and trained four postdocs, one staff scientist, 12 graduate students, and four undergraduates. Helped new group members settle into the lab and its research.
    • Ran day-to-day lab activities, identified training needs, and led weekly interdisciplinary meetings, workshops, research seminars, and a journal club.
    • Organised two annual lab retreats, each lasting two days, with career development and team-building activities. Taught and gave guest lectures in the School of Earth and Space Exploration and the School of Complex Adaptive Systems.

  • Jan 2022 – to Apr 2024

    Research Fellow in Theoretical Quantum Biology

    University of Surrey

    Department of Chemistry

    Guildford, UK

    I worked with Marco Sacchi on open quantum systems, non-Born–Oppenheimer effects, DNA damage, ion-channel transport, catalytic surfaces, and material systems.

    • Mentored and trained three graduate and four undergraduate students. As primary supervisor for two MPhys students, guided projects that each led to a paper and earned a grade in the top 5% of the cohort.
    • Trained researchers in programming, HPC, ab initio methods, DFT, machine-learning potentials, molecular dynamics, QM/MM, minimum-energy paths, enhanced sampling, and free-energy calculations.
    • Taught chemistry and physics, gave guest lectures and outreach talks, and mentored aspiring scientists.

  • Sep 2018 – to Jan 2022

    Postgraduate Researcher, Teaching Assistant, and Outreach Volunteer

    University of Surrey

    Quantum Biology Doctoral Training Centre

    Guildford, UK

    My doctoral research used computational chemistry and physics to investigate proton transfer and its role in how accurately DNA is copied.

    • Performed ab initio quantum chemistry calculations using national computing facilities including ARCHER, ARCHER2, and DIRAC.
    • Taught and trained undergraduates as a teaching assistant and laboratory demonstrator, and marked their work.
    • Set up a cross-faculty initiative to improve computing support and code literacy for PhD students while representing students in physics and chemistry.
    • Took part in science outreach through demonstrations, talks, and mentoring schemes.
